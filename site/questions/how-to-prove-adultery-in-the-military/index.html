How to prove adultery in the military

Adultery in the military is a crime under the Uniform Code of Military Justice if the service member is married. It requires proof of sexual intercourse between a married service member and someone who is not their spouse.

Published 6 November 2026 · Spry Labs

What counts as adultery under military law?

Adultery under the Uniform Code of Military Justice requires two elements. First, the service member must be married. Second, the service member must have sexual intercourse with someone who is not their spouse. The act must be official misconduct and prejudicial to good order and discipline.

The military does not prosecute every case of infidelity. Commanders decide whether to act based on whether the behavior harms unit cohesion or the service member's reputation. Civilian courts may also consider adultery in divorce or custody cases, but the standards differ from military law.

  • Marital status: the service member must be legally married
  • Sexual intercourse: physical act required
  • Not spouse: partner must be someone other than the service member's spouse
  • Official misconduct: behavior must affect good order and discipline

How is evidence gathered for an adultery case?

Evidence in military adultery cases often comes from witness statements, electronic communication, or observed behavior. Command investigators may interview fellow service members or review phone records. In civilian divorce or custody matters, screenshots, bank records, or hotel receipts may be used to show opportunity and inclination.

  • Electronic communication records

Can adultery affect a divorce or custody case?

In a civilian divorce, adultery may influence alimony or property division depending on state law. Some states are no-fault only, meaning marital misconduct is not considered. In custody cases, the court focuses on the child's best interest. Evidence of adultery may be relevant only if it affects the child's safety or stability.

Frequently asked questions

Can adultery charges be filed after a service member leaves the military?

Yes. The Uniform Code of Military Justice allows prosecution within two years of the offense. If the act occurred while the service member was on active duty, charges may be filed within that window even after separation.

Does adultery affect my VA disability rating?

No. Adultery is not a factor in VA disability rating decisions. Ratings are based on medical evidence of a service-connected condition. KeepMyProof is not affiliated with the VA and does not prepare or file claims.

Is adultery a crime in the military?

Yes. Adultery is a crime under the Uniform Code of Military Justice Article 134. It requires that the service member is married and has sexual intercourse with someone not their spouse, and that the conduct prejudices good order and discipline.
